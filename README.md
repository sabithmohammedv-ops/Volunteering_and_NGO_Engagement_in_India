# Volunteering and NGO Engagement in India

**AI-Powered Data Analysis Research Report**\
**Prepared by:** Sabith Mohammed V\
**Research area:** Civil society and volunteering

## 📌 Project Overview

This project explores volunteering and NGO engagement in India, with
particular attention to Kerala. It examines volunteering participation,
emerging trends, challenges faced by NGOs, and practical ways
organisations can engage and retain volunteers.

The research was organised with the support of AI tools to help
structure findings, compare figures, identify themes, and summarise
insights.

## 🎯 Objectives

-   Understand volunteering trends in India.
-   Collect and summarise relevant statistics from the research notes.
-   Identify how companies, young people, and communities contribute to
    volunteering.
-   Explore challenges NGOs face in engaging and retaining volunteers.
-   Suggest practical ways NGOs can improve volunteer programmes.

## 🔎 Key Trends Identified

1.  **Flexible volunteering:** Younger volunteers are interested in
    short-term, skill-based, and virtual opportunities.
2.  **Company-supported volunteering:** Corporate volunteering
    programmes are supported by companies' social responsibility
    activities.
3.  **Volunteers becoming donors:** Some volunteers later support NGOs
    through fundraising or donations.
4.  **Community-led volunteering in Kerala:** Examples discussed include
    the 2018 flood response, Haritha Karma Sena, and Kites Foundation.

## 💡 Key Insights

-   The reported Indian worker volunteering rate (31%) is higher than
    the global benchmark (22%).
-   Meaningful responsibilities can help volunteers stay engaged.
-   Volunteer retention is a challenge. A global review of community
    health volunteer programmes reported attrition ranging from 9% to
    53%, and up to 77% in some programmes. These figures relate to
    health programmes and should be treated as an indication, not a rule
    for all NGOs.
-   The report notes that about 91% of NGOs have budgets below ₹10
    crore, making affordable volunteer training and support important.

## 📈 How NGOs Can Measure Volunteer Programmes

NGOs can review their volunteer programmes by tracking:

-   Number of volunteers recruited.
-   Percentage of volunteers who continue after a defined period.
-   Volunteer hours contributed.
-   Skills gained by volunteers.
-   Number of volunteers who later donate or fundraise.
-   Volunteer feedback and level of support.

## 🧠 What This Research Demonstrates About Data Analysis

The project follows a simple analysis process:

**Data collection → Comparison → Trend identification → Insight
generation → Recommendations**

For example:

-   **Data:** 31% of Indian workers volunteered in 2024, compared with a
    22% global benchmark.
-   **Analysis:** The reported Indian figure is 9 percentage points
    higher.
-   **Insight:** Volunteering is a significant resource for NGOs in
    India.
-   **Recommendation:** NGOs can build on this participation by offering
    meaningful roles, flexible options, training, and support.
